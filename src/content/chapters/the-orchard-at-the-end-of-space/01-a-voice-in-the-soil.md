The apple tree had been dead for ninety-three years when it spoke to Elian Vale.

He was alone in Orchard Nine, ankle-deep in the black loam the *Pilgrim* carried from a planet nobody aboard had seen. Above him, the daylight panels dimmed through their evening sequence. The leaves of younger trees turned from brass to green to nearly black, and the long irrigation pipes ticked as they cooled.

Elian pressed two fingers into the soil beside the ancient trunk. The moisture reading was ordinary. The fungal web was healthy. Nothing in the orchard required him to be there after shift, except that he liked the hour when the machines became quiet enough to hear the small lives working.

That was when the voice rose through his gloves.

*You took your time.*

He jerked back, scattering soil across the path. Somewhere behind the western trellis, a pump sighed. Elian waited for the announcement chime—for a technician laughing over the maintenance channel, perhaps—but the orchard remained still.

He touched the ground again.

This time the voice did not speak. Instead, a memory opened behind his eyes: rain falling without permission, cold against his face; a sky so wide it made him dizzy; the smell of salt coming from an ocean beyond a hill.

Elian had never seen an ocean.

No one on the *Pilgrim* had.

When he pulled his hand away, there was a seed in his palm—small, silver, and warm as living skin.

From the corridor came the soft double-note of the night watch changing shifts. Elian closed his fist around the seed. The sensible thing was to call Biosecurity. The required thing was to seal Orchard Nine and record the anomaly.

Instead, for the first time in his careful life, he turned off the log recorder and lied to the ship.

“Routine inspection complete,” he said. “No irregularities.”
