By morning, the plant had covered half the cabin wall.

Its leaves formed an atlas in pieces: islands along one stem, mountain ranges branching from another, rivers brightening whenever Elian breathed near them. Sera photographed nothing. She wrote nothing down. For an archivist, this was more alarming than panic.

“The official maps begin with departure,” she said. “Earth is represented as coordinates and mass. Never landscape.”

Elian traced the coastline without touching it. “Then how do you recognize the rain?”

Sera watched the leaves turn slowly toward her. “Because memory survives editing differently than data.”

At the center of the plant, a new bud opened. It did not contain a flower. Suspended between its pale petals was a point of blue light, and around that point ran a string of navigation figures.

Elian read them twice.

“Those coordinates are behind us.”

“Yes.”

“How far?”

Sera’s silence answered first.

The *Pilgrim* had traveled for two hundred and eleven years toward Kepler-186f, carrying twelve thousand people and a promise that their descendants would wake beneath another sun. Every schoolroom showed the same bright animation: Earth diminishing behind the ship, the new world growing ahead.

But the blue point in the flower was neither Earth nor Kepler. It lay seven months behind them, in a region the navigation archive marked as empty.

Then the ship changed course.

It was barely perceptible—a tremor through the floor, water leaning in the glass on Elian’s desk—but every gardener knew the feel of artificial gravity correcting against a turn.

Sera gripped the wall. “Did you do that?”

The plant’s roots tightened around the cabin’s ventilation grate.

From every speaker on the ship, the ancient voice from the orchard whispered:

*Come back.*
